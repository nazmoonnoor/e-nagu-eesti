## Chapter 1 — Saame tuttavaks!
*(book pages 3–8 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-01-Saame-tuttavaks.md))*

**Teemad:** the Estonian language class · introducing yourself · `kas sa oled? / kas sul on?` · question words · `kas sulle meeldib? / kas sa oskad?`

**Grammatika:** the two basic question frames — `kas sa **oled** …?` (being) and `kas **sul on** …?` (having) — plus the core question words and `meeldib / oskan`.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This is the whole A2 speaking test in miniature: who you are, what you have, what you like, what you can do. Everything later is elaboration. If you can answer these four frames fluently and *ask* them back, you have the shape of the interview.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `Kas sa oled …?` and `Kas sul on …?` translate the same English "are you / do you have", but Estonian keeps them strictly apart — `sul on` is the possession frame and takes no verb "to have".

**Sõnavara väli:** greetings and introductions · classroom language · nationalities and languages.

## Chapter 2 — Kuidas palun?
*(book pages 9–12 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-02-Kuidas-palun.md))*

**Teemad:** asking what something means · spelling out an email address · reading your post · signs and notices

**Grammatika:** no new forms — a chapter of survival phrases: `Mida tähendab …? · Kuidas palun? · Kas te võiksite korrata?`

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Small chapter, disproportionate exam value. These are the phrases that keep a conversation alive when you don't understand, and the examiner is assessing whether you can *repair* a conversation, not whether you understood everything first time. Learn three of them cold.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Spelling aloud needs the Estonian letter names, not English ones — worth practising with your own name and email address, which you will certainly be asked for.

**Sõnavara väli:** clarification phrases · the alphabet spoken aloud · email and address vocabulary · public signs.

## Chapter 3 — Sinu kodu
*(book pages 13–20 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-03-Sinu-kodu.md))*

**Teemad:** where you live · which floor · rooms · things in the house · your dream house

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`mitmes?` — which number** | **kolmas** korrus *(the third floor)* |
| **`mitmendal?` — on which** | **kolmandal** korrusel *(on the third floor)* |
| **`mul on …` / `minu … on …`** | mul on suur köök · minu köök on suur |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The `mitmes` / `mitmendal` pair is the same distinction as dates (`esimene mai` vs `esimesel mail`), met here first with floors. Getting it now means the date forms later cost nothing.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `mitmes` names the floor, `mitmendal` says you are *on* it — English uses "third" for both, so this needs conscious separation.

**Sõnavara väli:** rooms and parts of a house · furniture basics · ordinals · describing where you live.

## Chapter 4 — Kui suur on üür?
*(book pages 21–27 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-04-Kui-suur-on-uur.md))*

**Teemad:** living in town vs in the country · rental adverts · what you do at home

**Grammatika:** the **kohakäänded** — the `kus?` location forms (`-s` / `-l`) applied to homes, towns and countryside.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Rental adverts are a realistic A2 reading text — abbreviated, number-dense, full of location endings. Practising on real ads is closer to the exam than practising on sentences.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `maal` (in the countryside) vs `maale` (to the countryside) vs `maalt` (from) — the `-maa` word takes `-l`, not `-s`, exactly as with `Saaremaal`.

**Sõnavara väli:** housing and rent · town and country · household activities · advert abbreviations.

## Chapter 5 — Mööbel ja kodumasinad
*(book pages 28–37 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-05-Moobel-ja-kodumasinad.md))*

**Teemad:** bathroom · living room · bedroom · kitchen · appliances · describing a room

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **III vorm (partitive) after `kaks`** | üks voodi → kaks voodi**t** → mul ei ole voodi**t** |
| **the three partitive shapes** | `-t` (riiuli**t**) · `-i` (kapp**i**) · `∅`/stem change (kella, lau**a**, patj**a**) |
| **`kus on asi?`** | location words for placing furniture |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This chapter quietly teaches the partitive through furniture, and the same form does three jobs at once: after a number, in negatives, and as a partial object. Learn each noun in all three forms (`voodi · voodi · voodit`) from here on — it is the single habit that pays off most across the whole exam.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The III vorm is not predictable: `kaks voodit` but `kaks vanni`, `kaks kappi`, `kaks lauda`, `kaks patja`. Memorise it with the word.

**Sõnavara väli:** furniture room by room · household appliances · placement and location words.

## Chapter 6 — Iseloom
*(book pages 38–42 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-06-Iseloom.md))*

**Teemad:** character adjectives · horoscopes · describing yourself and others · what an Estonian is like

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Adjective I / II / plural** | sõbralik — sõbraliku — sõbraliku**d** |
| **plural = II vorm + `-d`** | tark — targa — targa**d** · kurb — kurva — kurva**d** |
| **Superlative** | kes on **kõige sõbralikum**? |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Adjectives are the backbone of every description task, and the rule here is clean: **the plural is built on the II vorm, not the I vorm.** Once that clicks, plural agreement stops being guesswork.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The II vorm often changes the stem, so the plural looks nothing like the singular: `tark → targad`, `kurb → kurvad`, `arg → arad`, `laisk → laisad`, `rõõmus → rõõmsad`.

**Sõnavara väli:** character and personality adjectives · horoscope signs · opinion phrases.

## Chapter 7 — Perekond ja sugulased
*(book pages 43–51 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-07-Perekond-ja-sugulased.md))*

**Teemad:** family members · relatives · a family role-play

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`olema`** conjugated | olen · oled · on · oleme · olete · on |
| **`minul on …`** | **mul** on kaks õde |
| **Pronouns in three forms** | mina — minu — mind · sina — sinu — sind · tema — tema — teda |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The pronoun table in three forms is the reference you will keep returning to — subject, possessor, object. Family is also one of the most predictable A2 speaking topics, so this vocabulary is close to guaranteed value.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `minu` (my) and `mind` (me, object) are different jobs — `minu vend` vs `ta nägi mind`. And `tema` serves as both nominative and genitive, which makes it look like an exception.

**Sõnavara väli:** immediate family · extended relatives and in-laws · possession phrases.

## Chapter 8 — Palju õnne! Tähtpäevad
*(book pages 52–60 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-08-Palju-onne-tahtpaevad.md))*

**Teemad:** special days · national holidays · Independence Day · family traditions · greetings and good wishes

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Järgarvud (ordinals)** | esimene · teine · kolmas · neljas · kahekümnes |
| **Kuupäevad** | **teisel** veebruari**l** · **kahekümne neljandal** veebruari**l** |
| **Verbi pööramine** | full present-tense conjugation practice |
| **`Kellele?`** | kingin **emale** lilli |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Dates in the `millal?` form (`teisel veebruaril`) are asked in every A2 oral — your birthday, your arrival, a public holiday. Drill your own dates until they are automatic rather than learning the whole ordinal series.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Both parts take an ending: the ordinal goes `-ndal` and the month `-l` — `kahekümne neljandal veebruaril`. Estonians also write dates as `24.02.`, read aloud in the ordinal form.

**Sõnavara väli:** holidays and celebrations · ordinals · good wishes and congratulations · gift vocabulary.

## Chapter 9 — Välimus
*(book pages 61–65 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-09-Valimus.md))*

**Teemad:** eyes and hair · colours · face · opposites · paying compliments

**Grammatika:** plural agreement in practice — `tal on **pikad** juukse**d**`, `tal on **sinised** silma**d**`.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Describing a person is a standard speaking-test task, and this chapter is where plural agreement becomes automatic: hair and eyes are plural in Estonian, so both the adjective and the noun carry `-d`.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `juuksed` and `silmad` are normally plural — `mul on pikad juuksed`, never *pikk juus* for a hairstyle.

**Sõnavara väli:** hair, eyes and face · colours · appearance adjectives and their opposites · compliments.

## Chapter 10 — Pood on avatud
*(book pages 66–71 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-10-Pood-on-avatud.md))*

**Teemad:** kinds of shop · loyalty cards · weekdays · clock times · open or closed

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`-st … -ni`** | pood on avatud kümne**st** kuue**ni** |
| **weekday ranges** | esmaspäeva**st** reede**ni** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** `-st … -ni` covers opening hours, timetables, course dates and working hours — one pattern, constant use. Reading opening-hours signs is also a classic A2 reading item.

<!-- -->

## Chapter 11 — Ilmateade
*(book pages 72–75 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-11-Ilmateade.md))*

**Teemad:** the weather · wind and conditions · reading a forecast

**Grammatika:** weather verbs used impersonally — `vihma **sajab**`, `vihma **ei saja**`, `tuul **puhub**`.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Weather forecasts are a stock A2 listening text: numbers, directions and a small fixed vocabulary. It is one of the easiest listening tasks to prepare for completely, because the phrasing barely varies.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `vihma sajab` has no subject — literally "it rains rain", with `vihma` in the partitive. The negative is `vihma ei saja`, not *ei sajab*.

**Sõnavara väli:** weather conditions · temperature · wind and directions · forecast phrasing.

## Chapter 12 — Minu päev ja nädal
*(book pages 76–83 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-12-Minu-paev-ja-nadal.md))*

**Teemad:** your day · everyday activities · your working week

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`käin tööl` vs `lähen tööle`** | habitual `-l` vs one-off `-le` |
| **`-ti` habituals** | esmaspäevi**ti** · hommiku**ti** · õhtu**ti** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** *"Describe your typical day"* is one of the two or three most likely speaking prompts in the whole exam, and this chapter is built exactly around it. The `käima` / `minema` distinction is what makes it sound native: `käin tööl` (I work / go regularly) vs `lähen tööle` (I'm off to work now).

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The verb changes the case: `käin tööl` takes `-l`, `lähen tööle` takes `-le`. Using `minema` for a routine makes it sound like a one-time event.
>
> - `esmaspäeviti` (on Mondays, habitually) is not the same as `esmaspäeval` (on Monday, once).

**Sõnavara väli:** daily routine verbs · times of day · the working week · frequency expressions.

## Chapter 13 — Tule mulle külla!
*(book pages 84–88 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-13-Tule-mulle-kulla.md))*

**Teemad:** inviting someone · going and coming to visit · bringing something

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`mulle · sulle · talle`** | helista **mulle**! · tule **mulle** külla |
| **Conditional `-ks`** | ma mängi**ksin** *(full)* — ma mängi**ks** *(short)* |
| **making a command polite** | *Tule külla!* → *Kas sa **tuleks** külla?* |
| **`purk mett`** | quantity word + partitive |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The conditional as a **politeness device** is the takeaway. `Kas sa tuleks…?` instead of `Tule!` is the difference between issuing orders and making an invitation, and the speaking test is marked on register. In speech the short form (`tuleks`, `mängiks`) is used for every person — easier than it looks.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `külla minema` takes `-le` (`lähen sõbrale külla`) — the person you visit is the destination.
>
> - The short conditional is identical for all six persons; the long form is safer in writing.

**Sõnavara väli:** invitations and visiting · phone phrases · quantities and containers · polite requests.

## Chapter 14 — Kodused tööd
*(book pages 89–92 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-14-Kodused-tood.md))*

**Teemad:** household chores · saying what you have to do

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`pean` + `-ma`** | ma **pean** nõusid pese**ma** |
| **`mul on vaja` + `-da`** | **mul on vaja** nõusid pes**ta** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Two ways to say "I have to", each demanding a different infinitive. This is the cleanest possible illustration of the `-ma` / `-da` split that runs through the whole language — a two-line chapter that settles a recurring problem.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `pean` takes `-ma`, `on vaja` takes `-da`. They mean nearly the same thing, which is exactly why the wrong infinitive slips out.

**Sõnavara väli:** household chores and cleaning verbs · obligation phrases.

## Chapter 15 — Riided ja jalanõud
*(book pages 93–98 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-15-Riided-ja-jalanoud.md))*

**Teemad:** clothes · footwear and paired items · colours and patterns · compliments

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Plural II vorm + `-ga`** | sinis**te** sokki**dega** · rohelis**te** kinga**dega** |
| **`sobib / ei sobi`** | roosa seelik **sobib** siniste sokkidega |
| **`kandma`** | ma **kannan** sinist T-särki |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The plural comitative (`siniste sokkidega`) is the first place both words in a phrase have to carry plural case endings at once. It looks intimidating and is actually mechanical: plural II vorm on the adjective, `-dega` on the noun.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Footwear and paired garments are plural in Estonian: `püksid`, `prillid`, `kingad`, `sokid` — so they force plural agreement whether you want it or not.

**Sõnavara väli:** garments and footwear · colours and patterns · matching and suitability · compliments.

## Chapter 17 — Hobid
*(book pages 101–104 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-17-Hobid.md))*

**Teemad:** hobbies and free time

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`-mine` verbal noun** | tantsi**mine** · joonista**mine** — *mulle meeldib joonistamine* |
| **`käin -mas`** | käin tantsi**mas** · käin uju**mas** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Two high-value forms in four pages. `-mine` turns any verb into a noun so you can say what you *like* (`mulle meeldib lugemine`), and `käin -mas` says what you regularly *go and do*. Between them they answer the free-time question, which is guaranteed to come up.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `-mas` is a genuinely separate form, not a typo for `-ma`: `lähen ujuma` (I'm going to swim) vs `käin ujumas` (I go swimming regularly).

**Sõnavara väli:** hobbies, sports and pastimes · frequency expressions · liking and preferring.

## Chapter 18 — Mida sa eile tegid?
*(book pages 105–109 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-18-Mida-sa-eile-tegid.md))*

**Teemad:** yesterday and today · asking about the past

**Grammatika:** the **lihtminevik** (simple past) — `-sin · -sid · -s · -sime · -site · -sid`, and past questions.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The past tense is an A2 requirement, not an extra: both the speaking and the writing task ask you to narrate something that already happened. Learn the irregular ones as vocabulary — `läksin, olin, tulin, sain, jõin, sõin, tegin, nägin` — and the regular `-si-` verbs follow on their own.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The `ta` form drops the `-i-`: `ma käisin` but `ta käis`. And the common verbs have irregular stems that look nothing like the present: `lähen → läksin`, `teen → tegin`.

**Sõnavara väli:** past-time expressions (`eile, üleeile, eelmisel nädalal`) · everyday activity verbs in the past.

## Chapter 19 — Sõidan rongiga
*(book pages 110–114 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-19-Soidan-rongiga.md))*

**Teemad:** means of transport · routes from A to B · timetables

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`kust? kuhu?`** | **Tallinnast** **Tartusse** · **Eestist** **Austraaliasse** |
| **`-ga` for transport** | sõidan rongi**ga** · lennuki**ga** |
| **`mis kellaks?`** | pean kohal olema kella kolme**ks** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** `Kust? Kuhu?` as a matched pair, applied to a real journey, is the clearest way to fix the direction cases. And `-ga` for transport is asked directly — *"Kuidas sa tööle lähed?"*

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `mis kellaks?` takes `-ks` (by what time), not `-ni` (until what time) — `kella kolmeks` = by three, `kella kolmeni` = until three.

**Sõnavara väli:** vehicles and transport · departure and arrival · tickets and timetables · countries and cities in the direction cases.

## Chapter 20 — Ma ei koristanud
*(book pages 115–119 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-20-Ma-ei-koristanud.md))*

**Teemad:** what people did and didn't do · a Father Christmas story · a board game

**Grammatika:** the **past negative** — `ei` + `-nud`, positive → negative across all persons.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** A whole chapter on one form, and it is the easiest grammar win in the book: **`ei` + `-nud` never changes for person.** One form covers `ma, sa, ta, me, te, nad`. Learn it and half of past-tense production becomes automatic.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The negative uses the `-nud` stem, not the past stem: `ei läinud`, never *ei läksin*. The two stems often differ — `tegin` but `ei teinud`, `nägin` but `ei näinud`.

**Sõnavara väli:** everyday verbs in the past negative · time expressions for the past.

## Chapter 21 — Vaata, seal on siil!
*(book pages 120–123 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-21-Vaata-seal-on-siil.md))*

**Teemad:** wild animals · pets · insects · antonyms · comparing

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Comparative: II vorm + `-m`** | julge → julge**m** · suur → suure**m** · tark → targe**m** |
| **`kui` for comparison** | ma olen julge**m** **kui** karu |
| **Antonyms** | paired opposites for description |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Comparison is an explicit A2 task, and the rule is mechanical once the II vorm is right — which is the same reason chapter 6's adjective forms matter. The antonym pairs are efficient vocabulary: you learn two words per entry.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Some comparatives shift the stem vowel: `tark → targem`, `nõrk → nõrgem`, `arg → arem`, `laisk → laisem`. And comparison uses `kui`, which is the same word as "if/when".

**Sõnavara väli:** wild and domestic animals · insects · adjective pairs and opposites.

## Chapter 22 — Üks pilet palun!
*(book pages 124–128 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-22-Uks-pilet-palun.md))*

**Teemad:** cinema, concerts and courses · buying a ticket · reading event listings

**Grammatika:** `Kas sa oled kunagi …?` — the perfect used for experience, plus `millal sa viimati …?`

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Event listings are a realistic reading text — dates, times, prices, venues in compressed form. And *"Kas sa oled kunagi …?"* is the natural way an examiner opens a conversation about your experiences.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `kunagi` means "ever" in a question but "never" with a negative verb: `Kas sa oled kunagi …?` vs `ma ei ole kunagi …`.

**Sõnavara väli:** events and entertainment · tickets and booking · listings vocabulary.

## Chapter 23 — Amet ja töökoht
*(book pages 129–133 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-23-Amet-ja-tookoht.md))*

**Teemad:** professions · where you work · reading about people's jobs

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`kellena?` — `-na`** | töötan restoranis kokk**ana** |
| **`kus?` — the workplace** | töötan **restoranis** · **haiglas** · **koolis** |
| **plain profession** | minu vend **on** arhitekt |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** *"Kellena sa töötad?"* and *"Kus sa töötad?"* are two separate questions with two separate endings, and both are asked. `-na` for the role, `-s`/`-l` for the place: `töötan **koolis** **õpetajana**`.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** With `olema` the profession takes no ending (`ta on arhitekt`), but with `töötama` it takes `-na` (`ta töötab arhitektina`). The verb decides.

**Sõnavara väli:** professions and job titles · workplaces · work-related verbs.

## Chapter 24 — Curriculum vitae
*(book pages 134–139 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-24-Curriculum-vitae.md))*

**Teemad:** years and dates · your life story · a model CV · CV questions

**Grammatika:** **aastaarvud** — saying years — plus the life-event verbs (`sündisin, lõpetasin, kolisin, alustasin`) in the past.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This chapter assembles the past tense, dates and personal vocabulary into the single most exam-shaped task in the book: telling your own life story. Write your real CV in Estonian once and you have prepared simultaneously for the writing task and the biographical half of the interview.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Years are read as plain numerals after `aastal` (`aastal tuhat üheksasada üheksakümmend`), but the `millal?` form makes everything ordinal and genitive. Prepare your own key years in whichever form you find easier and stick to it.

**Sõnavara väli:** education and qualifications · employment history · life events · CV headings.

## Chapter 25 — Klaas piima
*(book pages 140–146 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-25-Klaas-piima.md))*

**Teemad:** food and drink · quantities and containers · foodstuffs · laying the table · a kama recipe

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Quantity word + partitive** | klaas piim**a** · tükk kook**i** · pakk või**d** · purk me**tt** |
| **Number + III vorm** | the container itself also takes `-t / -i` |
| **Käsk ja keeld** | sega! · ära sega! |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Food and quantities is a listed A2 topic, and this chapter is where the partitive finally has an obvious reason to exist: you are always talking about *some* of something. `Klaas piima` is not "a glass milk" — it is "a glass **of** milk", and the partitive is that "of".

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** When a number is added, **both** words take an ending: `kaks klaasi piima`, `kaks pakki võid`.
>
> - Mass nouns behave differently from countable ones — `piima`, `vett`, `mett` are always partial.

**Sõnavara väli:** food and drink · containers and quantities · tableware · recipe and cooking verbs.

## Chapter 26 — Mul on kõik korras!
*(book pages 147–149 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-26-Mul-on-koik-korras.md))*

**Teemad:** how you feel · words for wellbeing · how you react · emotions

**Grammatika:** no new forms — the `mul on …` frame applied to feelings, plus reaction phrases.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** *"Kuidas sul läheb?"* opens virtually every conversation, and a three-word answer is a wasted opportunity. This chapter gives you a range of replies and, more usefully, the reactions — `Tore! · Kahju! · Kuidas nii?` — that show you are actually listening.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Feelings use the `mul on` frame rather than "I am": `mul on hea tuju`, not *ma olen hea tuju*.

**Sõnavara väli:** moods and emotions · wellbeing phrases · reactions and sympathy.

## Chapter 27 — Mis sul viga on?
*(book pages 150–153 · **Sõnavara** · [open the chapter →](Settle-27-Mis-sul-viga-on.md))*

**Teemad:** what hurts · body parts · emergencies · common ailments · going to the pharmacy

**Grammatika:** the `mul valutab …` frame — `mul valutab pea`, `mu õlg valutab`.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Health is a listed A2 topic and the pharmacy or doctor role-play is a standard task. The vocabulary here is finite and predictable — body parts and half a dozen ailments — which makes it one of the more efficient topics to prepare.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** Estonian says the body part hurts, with the person in the `-l` form: `**mul** valutab pea`, literally "at me hurts the head".

**Sõnavara väli:** body parts · symptoms and ailments · pharmacy and emergency vocabulary.

## Chapter 28 — Mille üle te kaebate?
*(book pages 154–157 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-28-Mille-ule-te-kaebate.md))*

**Teemad:** at the doctor · describing when symptoms started · falling ill and getting better

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Polite/plural imperative `-ge / -ke`** | Töötage! · Lugege! · Ujuge! · Rääkige! |
| **Prohibition `Ärge …!`** | Ärge töötage! · Ärge lugege! |
| **`haigeks jääma` → `terveks saama`** | the `-ks` of changing state |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The `-ge/-ke` imperative is your **politeness register**: it is both the plural "you" and the polite singular. Any instruction to a stranger, an official or an examiner takes this form, so it matters well beyond the doctor's surgery.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** `-ge` or `-ke` depends on the stem — `lugege` but `ujuge`, `laulge` but `koristage`. It follows the `-da` infinitive, so learn it alongside that form.
>
> - The prohibition is `Ärge` + the same `-ge/-ke` form, not the bare stem.

**Sõnavara väli:** medical consultation language · symptoms over time · recovery · polite instructions.

## Chapter 29 — Kui ma oleks
*(book pages 158–161 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-29-Kui-ma-oleks.md))*

**Teemad:** polite questions · wishes and hypotheticals · what you'd like right now

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Conditional `-ks`, full form** | ma tahaksi**n** · sa tahaksi**d** · me tahaksi**me** |
| **short form — same for everyone** | ma / sa / ta / me / te / nad **tahaks** |
| **`kui ma oleks …`** | hypothetical framing |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This is chapter 13's politeness trick given a full treatment. Two jobs, both worth marks: **softening requests** (`Kas te aitaksite mind?`) and **hypotheticals** (`Kui ma oleks rikas…`). The short form is a gift — one ending for all six persons — so the entry cost is low.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** In speech the short form dominates, but in writing use the full personal endings; mixing registers within one sentence reads as an error.

**Sõnavara väli:** wishes and hypotheticals · polite requests · opinion and preference verbs.

## Chapter 30 — Mida tehakse?
*(book pages 162–167 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Settle-30-Mida-tehakse.md))*

**Teemad:** what people do indoors and outdoors · seasonal activities

**Grammatika**

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **The impersonal, positive** | siin jaluta**takse** · süü**akse** · loe**takse** |
| **The impersonal, negative** | **ei** jaluta**ta** · **ei** söö**da** · **ei** teh**ta** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The impersonal is the last major grammar item in the book and it is everywhere in written Estonian — signs, rules, news, general statements about what people do. You need to *recognise* it to pass the reading and listening papers; simple production is expected too.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.** The negative is not simply `ei` + the positive form — the `-kse` drops: `jalutatakse` → `ei jalutata`, `süüakse` → `ei sööda`, `tehakse` → `ei tehta`.
>
> - The form is built from the `-da` infinitive, so an irregular `-da` form gives an irregular impersonal.

**Sõnavara väli:** seasonal and leisure activities · indoor and outdoor verbs · the impersonal forms of common verbs.

*(book pages 168–173 · **Core A2** · [open the revision unit →](Settle-31-Kordamine.md))*

The book's single revision unit, covering all thirty chapters. Unlike *E nagu Eesti*, which revises every five chapters, *Settle* saves it all for the end — so treat this as a mock exam rather than a recap.

**What's in it**

| Osa | Sisu |
|-----|------|
| Vaata passi! | identity documents and personal details |
| Tee kompliment! | paying compliments |
| Rollimängud | role-plays across the book's situations |
| Lauamäng | a board game built on a large question board |
| Külm suvesupp · Üürile anda korter · Ilmateade | three reading tasks — a recipe, a rental ad, a forecast |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The three readings are the closest thing in either book to real A2 reading texts** — a recipe, a classified ad and a weather forecast, each in its own compressed register. Do them under time pressure.

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - There is only **one** revision unit for the whole book, so don't leave it until the last week — it works better as a diagnostic with time left to fix what it exposes.
