## Chapter 1 — Tere!
*(book pages 10–13 · **Recap** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-01-Tere.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Tervitused | greetings by time of day, goodbyes, `Kuidas läheb?` |
| Tähestik | the 9 vowels, each with a key word (`A nagu maa`) |
| Tutvumine | introducing yourself, asking someone's name |
| Eesti geograafia | the four regions, the 15 counties, `asub` |
| Asesõnad | personal pronouns, long vs short |
| Nägu ja keha | face and body parts |

**Keeletark** *(page 12)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **olema** conjugated | olen · oled · on · oleme · olete · **on** |
| **Present-tense personal endings** | `-n · -d · -b · -me · -te · -vad` → elan, elad, elab … |
| **Possessives** (long / short) | minu/mu · sinu/su · tema/ta · meie/me · teie/te · nende |
| **Negation** — verb drops its ending after `ei` | ma elan → ma **ei ela** (same form for everyone) |
| **Question words** | `Kas? · Kes? · Kus? · Mis?` |
| **"Where?" = `-s` vs `-l`** | Tartu**s** · Narva**s** — but Saaremaa**l** · Sillamäe**l** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** The `-s` / `-l` split is the seed of the whole locative-case system (*sisseütlev, seesütlev, alaltütlev* …) that A2 leans on hardest. Lock the rule down here and chapters 3, 5 and 14 become much easier.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - Place names in **-maa, -mäe, -järve** take **-l**, not -s: `Saaremaal`, `Sillamäel`, `Kohtla-Järvel`. Everything else defaults to `-s`.
> - **olema** uses `on` for *both* `tema` and `nemad` — there is no `*olevad`.
> - `Õ Ä Ö Ü` are separate letters, not decorated O/A/U.

**Sõnavara väli:** times of day · greeting formulas · body parts · adjectives (`ilus, vana, tark, hea, tubli`) · verbs `olema, elama, asuma, armastama`.

## Chapter 2 — Maad ja keeled
*(book pages 14–17 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-02-Maad-ja-keeled.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Euroopa kaart | the Baltic states, `Läänemere ääres`, capitals |
| Rahvused | nationality words and the `-lane / -lased` suffix |
| Keeleoskus | saying which languages you know and how well |
| Päritolu | `Kust sa pärit oled?` · `Kus sa oled sündinud?` |
| Õpetaja ja õpilane | `õpetama` vs `õppima`, and the `-ja / -lane / -k` word family |
| Suur ja väike täht | when to capitalise |

**Keeletark** *(page 16)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Present tense of stem-changing verbs** | lugema → **loen** · rääkima → **räägin** · õppima → **õpin** |
| **`Kus?` / `Kust?`** — the four endings paired | `-s → -st` (Eestis → Eestist) · `-l → -lt` (Saaremaal → Saaremaalt) |
| **Multi-word verbs** | `pärit olema` · `aru saama` · `tuttavaks saama` |
| **`olema` + nationality / origin**, with the `-nud` form | olen eestlane · olen **sündinud** Jõhvis · olen **õppinud** vene keelt |
| **Riik · pealinn · keel · rahvus** table | Eesti — Tallinn — eesti keel — eestlane — eestlased |
| **Capitalisation** | **Eesti**, **Tallinn**, **Karl Kivi** — but *eesti keel*, *eestlane* |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** Three things pay off immediately:
>
> 1. **Partitive object with languages** — `keel + t`: *Ma õpin / räägin / oskan eesti **keelt***, never *keel*. This is your first real object-case rule.
> 2. **The `kus / kust` pair** — the A2 speaking test opens with `Kust sa pärit oled?` almost every time.
> 3. **The `-nud` participle** in `olen sündinud` / `olen õppinud` — the perfect tense arriving early. Chapter 20 and grammar ch. 15 formalise it; recognise it now.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - `-maa / -mäe / -järve` words take **-lt** in the *kust?* form: `Saaremaalt`, not *Saaremaast*.
> - **õpetama** (to teach) vs **õppima** (to learn) — one letter apart, and examiners use it.
> - `eesti keel` lowercase, `Eesti` the country capitalised. Same for `venelane` / `Venemaa`.

**Sõnavara väli:** countries, capitals, languages, nationalities · `emakeel` · study verbs `oskama, rääkima, lugema, kirjutama, õppima` · hedging adverbs `natuke, päris, kahjuks, tõesti, veel, juba, ainult` (very useful in the speaking test).

## Chapter 3 — Kus sa elad?
*(book pages 18–21 · **Recap** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-03-Kus-sa-elad.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Aadress | address format, abbreviations `t/tn · pst · mnt · hr · pr · lp` |
| Numbrid | 11 → 1 000 000, plus `pluss · miinus · korda`, `õige / vale` |
| Kontakt | phone numbers, email, `helista · ütle · saada` |
| Linnaosad | districts of Tallinn and Tartu — more `-s` / `-l` practice |
| Tallinn ja Tartu | two short city readings, populations |
| Kirjavahemärgid | `täht · sõna · lause`, `punkt · koma · hüüumärk · küsimärk` |

**Keeletark** *(page 20)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **The four pronoun forms**: `kes? · kelle? · kellel? · kellele?` | mina — **minu** korter — **mul** on maja — helista **mulle** |
| **Numbers: one word or two** | 13 kolm**teist** · 30 kolm**kümmend** — but 33 kolmkümmend **kolm** |
| **Reading numbers aloud** | addresses (`138–45`), phone numbers, plain figures |
| **Imperative, `sa` and `te`** | ma loe**n** → **Loe!** / **Lugege!** · ma vasta**n** → **Vasta!** / **Vastake!** |
| **Punctuation names** | punkt `.` · koma `,` · hüüumärk `!` · küsimärk `?` |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The `mul / mulle` series** is the adessive and allative doing their two most common jobs: possession (`mul on …`) and liking (`mulle meeldib …`). Estonian has no verb "to have", so this pattern carries every sentence about what you own, and it comes back formally in chapter 4.
> 2. **Imperatives** — the A2 exam asks you to give instructions and make polite requests; the `sa`/`te` split is also your politeness register.
> 3. **Numbers read aloud** — prices, times, addresses and phone numbers are staple A2 listening material.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - Don't confuse **omastav** `minu korter` (my flat) with **adessive** `mul on korter` (I have a flat) — same pronoun, different job.
> - The `te` imperative is usually **-ge** but becomes **-ke** after certain stems: `vasta**ke**`, `saat**ke**`. It follows the *da*-infinitive, so learn it with the TS forms.
> - `ütlema` has two accepted imperatives: `Ütelge!` = `Öelge!`

**Sõnavara väli:** address vocabulary (`tänav, puiestee, maantee, vald, küla`) · numbers and arithmetic · contact verbs (`helistama, saatma, küsima, vastama`) · honorifics `hr / pr / lp`.

## Chapter 4 — Üks ja mitu
*(book pages 22–25 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-04-Uks-ja-mitu.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Mitu? | number + noun — `kaks kassi`, `viis tüdrukut` |
| Ainsus ja mitmus | the plural `-d`, and adjectives agreeing with it |
| Pere | `laps · sõber · tütar · poeg · õde · vend` and their irregular plurals |
| Värvid | colours in singular and plural, `tumehall · helekollane` |
| See / need | pointing at one thing vs many |
| Meeldib | saying what you like, and who likes it |

**Keeletark** *(page 25)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **The three basic forms** — nimetav · omastav · osastav | **eesti keel** on ilus — **eesti keele** õpik — ma õpin **eesti keelt** |
| **Ainsus → mitmus**, adjective agrees | tark poiss → **targad poisid** (built on the omastav stem) |
| **Number + osastav singular** | kaks **tarka poissi** · kaks **väikest tüdrukut** |
| **The three partitive groups** | `-t` (tänava**t**) · `∅` (linna, venda) · `-d` (kuu**d**, öö**d**) |
| **`ei ole` / `pole` + osastav** | Mul ei ole **kassi** · Tal pole **ratast** |
| **Kelle? chains of omastav** | minu **venna** maja · tema **õe** korter |
| **Kellel on / ei ole** | Mul **on** neli kassi · Emal **ei ole** koera |
| **Kellele meeldib / meeldivad** | Mulle **meeldib** punane · Mulle **meeldivad** roosid |
| **Negative imperative `ära / ärge`** | **Ära** vaata! · **Ärge** vaadake! |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This is the most important chapter of the first five — nearly everything later is built on it.
>
> 1. **nimetav · omastav · osastav.** Learn every noun in all three forms from here on. The omastav is the stem that *every* other case ending attaches to (`venna` → `vennal, vennale, vennast, vennaga`), so getting it wrong breaks the whole word.
> 2. **Number + partitive singular** — `kaks kassi`, `viis tüdrukut`. Guaranteed to appear.
> 3. **Negation takes the partitive** — `mul ei ole autot`. The A2 written test punishes `*mul ei ole auto`.
> 4. **`meeldima`** is the standard way to express preference in the speaking test, and its agreement rule is counter-intuitive.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - After a number of 2 or more the noun stays **singular partitive**, never plural: `kaks kassi`, not *kaks kassid*.
> - Which partitive group a word belongs to (`-t` / nothing / `-d`) is **not predictable** — memorise it with the word, as part of the three-form set.
> - `meeldib` / `meeldivad` agrees with the **thing liked**, not the person: *Mulle meeldi**vad** roosi**d***.
> - Plural agreement is doubled — both the adjective and the noun take `-d`: `suured loomad`.
> - Irregular family plurals: `laps → lapsed` but `kaks **last**`; `sõber → sõbrad` but `kaks **sõpra**`.

**Sõnavara väli:** quantity and number words · family members · colours (incl. `tume-` / `hele-` compounds) · animals · `meeldima, arvama, muretsema`.

## Chapter 5 — Kuhu sa lähed?
*(book pages 28–33 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-05-Kuhu-sa-lahed.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Kellaaeg | telling the time — `veerand · pool · kolmveerand · täpselt` |
| Nädalapäevad | weekdays, `üleeile → ülehomme`, `nädalavahetus` |
| Sõidukid | transport, and going somewhere `bussiga · rattaga · jala` |
| Bussijaamas | timetables, buying tickets, paying `kaardiga / sularahas` |
| Kaugused | distances between Estonian towns |
| Taksos | ordering and taking a taxi |
| Taimi Päev | a reading tying together times, routes and family |

**Keeletark** *(page 32)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Multi-word verbs** | ma tõuse**n üles** · ma jää**n hiljaks** — the particle stays put |
| **Imperative, incl. negative** | **Tõuse** üles! · **Ära jää** hiljaks! · **Saage tuttavaks!** |
| **Irregular stems** | minema → ma **lähen** · sõitma → ma **sõidan** |
| **The full `kuhu? / kus? / kust?` triple** | Tartu**sse** — Tartu**s** — Tartu**st** · Saaremaa**le** — Saaremaa**l** — Saaremaa**lt** |
| **Short "to" forms** | Narva · Võrru · Haapsa**llu** · Rakve**rre** · **koju** · kooli · linna |
| **Millega? / Kellega? — `-ga`** | rongi**ga** · sõbra**ga** (on the omastav stem) |
| **Millal? — `-l`** | hommiku**l** · õhtu**l** · esmaspäeva**l** · nädalavahetuse**l** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The complete kuhu / kus / kust triple** is the highest-yield grammar in the first five chapters. Chapters 1 and 2 gave you two thirds of it; here the "to" column arrives and the system closes. Direction is tested in every part of the exam.
> 2. **`-ga` on the omastav stem** answers both *with whom* and *by what transport* — `Kellega sa lähed?` and `Millega sa tuled?` are standard speaking-test questions.
> 3. **Time expressions**: `-l` for when (`esmaspäeval`, `hommikul`) plus clock time. A2 listening is built on schedules, departure times and prices.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - The short "to" forms are **irregular and must be learned as vocabulary**: `Narva`, `Võrru`, `Haapsallu`, `Rakverre`, `Kuressaarde`, `Tartusse` — no single rule covers them.
> - **`koju`** (to home) is irregular — never *kodusse*. Compare `kodus` (at home) and `kodust` (from home).
> - Estonian half-hours **count forward**: `pool seitse` = 6:30, not 7:30. Likewise `veerand viis` = 4:15.
> - `iga päev` takes **no** `-l`, even though `igal hommikul` and `igal reedel` do.
> - `tund` means both *hour* and *lesson* — context decides.

**Sõnavara väli:** clock and calendar · vehicles and travel · tickets, timetables, payment · distances · motion verbs (`minema, tulema, sõitma, tõusma, jääma`) · the `siia / siin / siit` and `sinna / seal / sealt` sets.

> 1. **The Küsisõnad table is the single best speaking-test revision page in the book** — the 18 question words map almost one-to-one onto the prompts an A2 examiner uses. Be able to both answer *and* ask each one.
> 2. **Grammar summary block 4** states the rule the whole case system rests on: every other ending attaches to the **omastav** stem (`vend → venna → vennal, vennale, vennast, vennaga`). If one thing from chapters 1–5 goes into long-term memory, make it this.
> 3. **The 46 väikesed sõnad** are what lift A2 writing from correct to natural — `küll, vist, muidugi, päris, tavaliselt, võib-olla`. Cheap marks, easily overlooked.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - **Tests A and B have no answer key** in the book — self-check against the chapter pages.
> - Word pairs are examinable as pairs: `edasi ↔ tagasi`, `vara ↔ hilja`, `siia–sinna`, `siin–seal`.
> - `vist` and `võib-olla` are interchangeable; `Ära mine!` (sa) vs `Ärge minge!` (te) is not.

## Chapter 6 — Aastaajad
*(book pages 46–51 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-06-Aastaajad.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Neli aastaaega | weather in each season, `päike paistab`, `vihma/lund sajab` |
| Kuud | the twelve months, grouped by season |
| Temperatuur | `10 kraadi sooja` / `10 kraadi külma` |
| Reisimine | which month → which country, travel adverts, holiday dialogues |
| Mis on head kevadel…? | what's good about each season — nature, flowers, school year |
| Soovid | set wishes: `Head reisi! · Häid jõule! · Palju õnne sünnipäevaks!` |

**Keeletark** *(page 50)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Millal? — the ending depends on the group** | seasons `-l` (talve**l**) · months **`-s`** (jaanuari**s**) · weekdays `-l` (reede**l**) · times of day `-l` (hommiku**l**) |
| **Verb + `-ma` or `-da`** | minema/tulema/sõitma + **`-ma`** (lähme jaluta**ma**) · oskama/tahtma/meeldima + **`-da/-ta/-a`** (mulle meeldib uju**da**) |
| **Kuhu? with countries and cities** | `-sse` (Itaalia**sse**) · `Ø` (Soome, Rootsi, **Lätti**, **Leetu**) · `-le` (Saksamaa**le**, Küprose**le**) |
| **minema kuhu · käima kus · tulema kust** | lähen Londoni**sse** · olen käinud Itaalia**s** · tuleb Riia**st** tagasi |
| **Komad** | comma **before** `et · kus · mis · sest · kui` — **no** comma before `ja · ning · ega · või` |
| **Väike täht** | seasons, months, weekdays and holidays stay lowercase |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The `-ma` / `-da` infinitive split** is the single highest-frequency error source from here on, and it is grammatical rather than lexical: the *verb in front* decides. Motion and starting verbs take `-ma`; wanting, knowing-how and liking take `-da`. Learn it as two lists.
> 2. **Months take `-s`, everything else takes `-l`.** Small table, constant payoff — every question about dates, seasons and schedules runs through it.
> 3. **Comma rules.** A2 writing is marked on punctuation, and the missing comma before `sest` and `et` is the most common single deduction.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - Months break the pattern: `jaanuari**s**` but `talve**l**`, `reede**l**`, `hommiku**l**`. This is the one inconsistency in the set.
> - Country "to" forms have **no rule** — `-maa` countries take `-le` (`Saksamaale`), but the near neighbours use a bare short form (`Soome`, `Rootsi`, `Lätti`, `Leetu`). Memorise them.
> - Seasons, months, weekdays and holiday names are **lowercase** in Estonian — the opposite of English.
> - No comma before `ja` / `ning` / `ega` / `või`, however long the clause.

**Sõnavara väli:** seasons and weather · months · nature (`mets, järv, meri, lind, lumi, päike`) · travel and holidays · fixed good-wish phrases. The chapter's Sõnavara list gives all three forms (nimetav · omastav · osastav) — use it that way.

| Sugulased | the wider relatives — `onu, tädi, äi, ämm, abikaasa` |

**Keeletark** *(page 55)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Vanus** — numeral omastav + `-aastane` | kahe**aastane** · viie**aastane** · kahekümne ühe**aastane** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**

> 2. **Adjective agreement in the cases**: `suures majas`, not *suur majas*. The adjective copies the noun's ending, and examiners look for it.
> 3. **Age** needs the numeral's genitive — `kahe-`, `kolme-`, `viie-` — so the irregular numeral forms have to be solid before `-aastane` works.

<!-- -->

>
> - Postpositions come **after** the noun, the mirror of English: `maja ees` = *in front of the house*.

> - `abielluma` (to get married, an event) vs `abielus olema` (to be married, a state) — different questions follow each (`kellega? millal?` vs `kellega? kui kaua?`).
> - The `pärast` / `läbi` clock idiom reverses English word order: *ten minutes past two* = `kümme minutit kaks läbi`.

**Sõnavara väli:** house and rooms · immediate family and in-laws · ages and numerals · location words · daily evening vocabulary.

## Chapter 8 — Huvid ja hobid
*(book pages 58–63 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-08-Huvid-ja-hobid.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Huvid ja hobid | naming hobbies and interests |
| Mulle meeldib | saying what you and others like doing |
| Käin kus? Lähen kuhu? | the habitual `käima` vs the one-off `minema` |
| Nädalavahetuse plaanid | making weekend plans |
| Sport | sporting family, `trenn`, sports vocabulary |
| Tegija ja tegevus | agent and activity nouns (`laulja / laulmine`) |
| Ma ei saa täna trenni tulla | declining an invitation, giving a reason |

**Keeletark** *(page 61)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Object pronouns (osastav)** | **mind · sind · teda · meid · teid · neid** — Aita **mind**! Ma ootan **sind**. |
| **Millal? with `sel / järgmisel / igal`** | **sel** kolmapäeval · **järgmisel** suvel · **igal** aastal |
| **Lähen kuhu?** three columns | `-sse` (teatri**sse**) · `-le` (töö**le**, peo**le**) · short form (kooli, parki, trenni, **tuppa**, **majja**, **kinno**, **koju**) |
| **tegema – teha · nägema – näha** | mida sa tahad **teha**? · ma tahan seda **näha** |
| **kuulama vs kuulma** | ma **kuulan** raadiot *(listen)* · ma ei **kuule** hästi *(hear)* |
| **minu vs oma** | see on **minu** auto — but ma sõidan **oma** perega |
| **sest vs seepärast** | mulle meeldib suvi, **sest** … · ilm on ilus, **seepärast** … |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **`oma`.** Whenever the possessor is also the subject of the sentence, Estonian requires `oma`, not `minu/sinu/tema`. It is a small word that marks a learner out immediately, and it appears in almost every sentence about your own life.
> 2. **Object pronouns** `mind / sind / teda` — needed the moment a person is on the receiving end of a verb, which in the speaking test is constantly.
> 3. **The short illative** (`kooli`, `linna`, `trenni`, `tuppa`, `koju`) is the form you actually hear; the `-sse` form often sounds bookish for these words.
> 4. **`sest` vs `seepärast`** gives you cause *and* consequence — two connectors that visibly raise the level of A2 writing.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - `Ma sõidan **oma** perega`, never *minu perega*, when "I" am the subject.
> - `kuulama` (listen) vs `kuulma` (hear) — one letter, and both are common.
> - `igal aastal` and `igal reedel` take `-l`, but **`iga päev`** does not.
> - Several short illatives double a consonant: `tuba → tuppa`, `maja → majja`, `kino → kinno`.
> - `käima` is habitual (`ma käin trennis`), `minema` is this one occasion (`ma lähen trenni`) — and note the case changes with the verb.

**Sõnavara väli:** hobbies and sports · places you go regularly · agent nouns in `-ja` and activity nouns in `-mine` · invitation and refusal phrases.

## Chapter 9 — Hommikul kodus
*(book pages 64–69 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-09-Hommikul-kodus.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Hommikused tegevused | the morning routine, verb by verb |
| Minevik | the past tense introduced — "what already happened" |
| Hommikusöök | breakfast foods and drinks |
| Mida sa sõid? Mida sa jõid? | talking about what you ate and drank |
| Dialoogid | breakfast-table conversations |
| Köök | kitchen things — `tass, taldrik, nuga, kahvel, lusikas, pann` |

**Keeletark** *(page 67)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **The `-si-` past (imperfect)** | ma käi**sin** · sa käi**sid** · ta käi**s** · me käi**sime** · te käi**site** · nad käi**sid** |
| **Irregular pasts to learn by heart** | lähen→**läksin** · olen→**olin** · tulen→**tulin** · saan→**sain** · joon→**jõin** · söön→**sõin** · teen→**tegin** · näen→**nägin** |
| **Past negative `ei` + `-nud`** | ei käi**nud** · ei läi**nud** · ei tei**nud** — one form for every person |
| **`-ni`** (until) | loeng oli kella **kaheni** · olen koolis **poole viieni** |
| **`ilma …-ta`** | kohvi **ilma suhkruta** · ilma **mütsita** |
| **Partitive objects** | sõi **putru** · jõin **vett** · lugesime **ajalehte** · tegid **pannkooki** |
| **kohtuma + `-ga`** | ma kohtusin oma sõbra**ga** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.** This is the biggest grammar chapter so far — budget extra time for it.
>
> 1. **The past tense is an A2 requirement, not a bonus.** Both the speaking and the writing task ask you to narrate something that already happened ("what did you do at the weekend?"). Without it you cannot pass those parts.
> 2. **Learn the irregular column as vocabulary**, not as a rule: `läksin, olin, tulin, sain, jõin, sõin, tegin, nägin, panin, pidin`. The regular `-si-` verbs then take care of themselves.
> 3. **The negative past is a free win** — `ei` + `-nud` never changes for person. One form covers all six.
> 4. **Partitive objects with food verbs**: eating and drinking are partial actions, so `sõin putru`, `jõin vett`. This is your first systematic encounter with object case being a *meaning* choice.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - The negative past uses the **`-nud` stem, not the `-si-` stem**: `ei läinud`, never *ei läksin*. And it is invariant — `ma/sa/ta/me/te/nad ei läinud`.
> - The three stems of one verb can all differ: `lähen → läksin → ei läinud`; `teen → tegin → ei teinud`.
> - The `ta` form is `-s` for `-si-` verbs (`ta käis`) but `-i` for the irregulars (`ta oli`, `ta tuli`, `ta jõi`).
> - Weather verbs shift too: `vihma sajab` → `vihma **sadas**`.

**Sõnavara väli:** morning-routine verbs · food and drink · kitchen and tableware · time expressions for the past (`eile, üleeile, eelmisel nädalal, kaks nädalat tagasi`).

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**

**Keeletark** *(page 93)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Comparative** — omastav + `-m` | kallis → kalli**m** · odav → odava**m** · maitsev → maitsva**m** |
| **Superlative** — `kõige` + comparative | **kõige** kallim · **kõige** väiksem |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**

<!-- -->

>
> - The irregular comparatives are the frequent ones — `hea → parem`, `palju → rohkem`, `vähe → vähem`, `pikk → pikem`, `lühike → lühem`. No rule; learn the list.

> - `mõtlema` and `ütlema` each have two accepted forms: `mõtelda = mõelda`, `ütelda = öelda`.

| Teema | Sisu |

| Maja | parts of a house and building |
| Kinnisvarakuulutused | reading property sale and rental ads |
| Korterit üürima | the rental conversation — rent, deposit, moving in |
| Naabrid | neighbours and getting on with them |
| Vana ja uus kodu | comparing where you lived and where you live now |
| Korrused | which floor, `mitmendal korrusel` |

**Keeletark** *(page 106)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Plural genitive `-te / -de`** | uue korteri → uu**te** korteri**te** · hea naabri → hea**de** naabri**te** |
| **Full plural declension** | uue**d** korteri**d** — uu**te** korteri**te** — uus**i** kortere**id** |
| **`-ga` in the plural** | naabri**te**ga · kodumasina**te**ga |
| **Adjective suffixes `-lik` / `-line`** | õnne**lik** päev · kahetoa**line** korter · suu**line** eksam |
| **Noun suffixes `-line` / `-nik` / `-ja`** | üüri**line** · üür**nik** · oma**nik** · müü**ja** · ela**nik** |
| **`mitmendal korrusel?`** | esime**sel** · tei**sel** · kolma**ndal** · viie**ndal** korrusel |
| **viima vs tooma** | **viima** siit → sinna *(take away)* · **tooma** siia ← sealt *(bring here)* |
| **huvitama keda?** | **mind** huvitab ajalugu · mis **sind** huvitab? |
| **Phrasal verbs** | sisse/välja kolima · välja üürima · läbi saama · kaasa tooma |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The plural genitive is the gateway to every plural case form.** Once you can make `uute korterite`, all the plural endings hang off it — `uutes korterites`, `uutele korteritele`, `uute korteritega`. Miss it and the whole plural collapses.
> 2. **`viima` vs `tooma`** — direction is built into the Estonian verb, and English "take/bring" maps onto it badly. Worth conscious drilling.
> 3. **The word-formation suffixes** (`-lik, -line, -nik, -ja`) let you decode unfamiliar words in the reading test rather than looking them up — high value for the exam's reading section.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - `-te` or `-de` depends on the stem: `uu**te**` but `hea**de**`, `valge**te**` but `tubli**de**`. There is no single rule — learn it with the word type.
> - `viima` and `tooma` are fixed relative to the **speaker's** position, not the subject's.
> - Floors reuse the date ordinals: `kolmandal korrusel`, same form as `kolmandal märtsil`.
> - `üüriline` (tenant, the person renting) vs `üürnik` (also tenant) vs `omanik` (owner) — the ad language uses these precisely.

**Sõnavara väli:** housing and property · rental and contract language · neighbours · building and floor vocabulary · adjective and agent suffixes.

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Object case — unfinished / negative / "some"** → **osastav** | kirjutan praegu **artiklit** · võtke veel **leiba** · meil ei ole **küsimusi** |
| **Object case — finished** → **nimetav** (pl) / **omastav** (sg) | kirjutasin **artikli** valmis · sõime kõik **koogid** ära |
| **Positive vs negative commands** | tehke **uks** lahti! — ärge tehke **aknaid** lahti! |

| **jõudma · unustama · kolima kuhu?** | jõudsin **koju** · unustasin võtmed **koju** · kolisid **maale** |
| **palju vs paljud** | **palju** eesti **toite** *(+ partitive)* — **paljud** eesti **toidud** *(+ nominative)* |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **This is the object case (sihitis) in full, and it is the hardest thing in Estonian grammar.** The choice between total object (nimetav/omastav) and partial object (osastav) carries meaning, not just form: *finished* vs *ongoing*. A2 tests it, and it is where most learners plateau. Give this section more time than any other page in the book so far.
> 2. **The postposition sets now mirror `kuhu / kus / kust`** — `peale / peal / pealt`. Same three-way logic as the case endings, so you get them almost free.
> 3. **`palju` + partitive vs `paljud` + nominative** is a clean minimal pair and an easy exam point.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - Same verb, different case, different meaning: `Ma kirjutan **artiklit**` (I'm writing an article, in progress) vs `Ma kirjutasin **artikli**` (I wrote it, finished).
> - **Negative sentences always take the partitive object** — no exceptions to memorise, which makes this the one easy rule in the area.
> - Positive commands take the total object (`Tee uks lahti!`), negative commands the partitive (`Ärge tehke aknaid lahti!`).
> - `laua peale` and `lauale` mean the same thing — the postposition and the case ending are interchangeable, so don't hunt for a difference.

**Sõnavara väli:** furniture and household objects · rooms · position and placement words · verbs of arriving, forgetting and moving.

**Keeletark** *(page 117)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **Two past tenses side by side** | *mida tegi?* unusta**sin** — *mida on teinud?* **olen** unusta**nud** |
| **Both negatives** | ma **ei unustanud** — ma **ei ole** unustanud |
| **Visiting verbs, each with its own case** | külla minema **kellele** (vanemate**le**) · külas käima **kellel** (sugulas**tel**) · külastama **keda** |
| **`on` + adjective + `-da`** | **on raske** sõpru **leida** · **on mõnus** **istuda** |
| **rääkima / mõtlema millest?** | räägin oma pere**st** · mõtlen töö**st** ja vaba**st** aja**st** |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The perfect (`olen teinud`) against the simple past (`tegin`).** The perfect is for experience and present relevance — *"I have forgotten what that word means"*, *"I have been to Italy"*. The simple past is for a finished event at a stated time. A2 expects you to produce both and to choose correctly.
> 2. **`rääkima / mõtlema millest`** — the `-st` case for topics. Every speaking prompt is some version of *"räägi oma perest / oma tööst / oma reisist"*, so this ending is the one you use to start almost every answer.
> 3. **`on raske / on mõnus / on huvitav` + `-da`** is a ready-made sentence frame for opinions, which the writing task rewards.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - **`pidama` is two verbs in one**, and the pasts differ: `pidime` (had to) vs `pidasime` (celebrated/held).
> - The visiting verbs each demand a different case — `lähen vanematele külla` (`-le`), `käisin sugulastel külas` (`-l`), `külastasin haiglat` (partitive). Mixing them is a classic error.
> - `külastama` is formal, for official visits. In everyday speech use `käia külas` — using `külastama` about your grandmother sounds like a press release.
> - `-ki` vs `-gi` is decided purely by the preceding sound, not by meaning.

**Sõnavara väli:** hosting and visiting · invitations and greetings at the door · gifts · recipes and cooking verbs · polite phrases.

> - `paljud` + nominative plural vs `palju` + partitive returns from chapter 14: `paljud juhid` but `palju juhte`.

**Sõnavara väli:** breakdowns and repairs · emergency and police vocabulary · accidents and warnings · quantifier pronouns · two-word verbs of state change.

*(book pages 172–177 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-21-Uks-toopaev.md))*

| Mis on sulle tööl oluline? | what matters to you at work |

**Keeletark** *(page 176)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **`mis aastal?` — plain numeral** | **aastal** tuhat üheksasada seitsekümmend neli |
| **`millal?` — ordinal, everything in genitive** | **tuhande üheksasaja seitsekümne neljandal** aastal |
| **Sajandid** | **kahekümne esimesel** sajandil |

| **hakkama + `-ma` / alustama + noun** | **hakkasin töötama** 2007 — **alustasin tööd** 2007 |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **Years complete the date system.** `Mis aastal?` takes the plain numeral; `millal?` puts every part into the ordinal genitive — `tuhande üheksasaja seitsekümne neljandal aastal`. It is a mouthful, so drill the two or three years you actually need (birth, arrival in Estonia, starting your job).
> 2. **Verb government, again, and in opposite directions**: `küsima` takes `-lt` (ask *from*), `vastama` takes `-le` (answer *to*).
> 3. **Work is a listed A2 speaking topic**, and this chapter supplies both the vocabulary and the sentence frames for talking about your own job.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - In the ordinal year form, **every element** goes into the genitive, not just the last one: `tuhande üheksasaja seitsekümne neljandal`.
> - `küsima kellelt` vs `vastama kellele` — getting these backwards is a frequent error.
> - `alguses / keskel / lõpus` need the preceding noun in the **omastav**.
> - The `algama / alustama` pair from chapter 10 returns here — intransitive vs transitive, and by now it should be automatic.

**Sõnavara väli:** office and workplace · job skills and duties · meetings and reports · years, centuries and periods · job-satisfaction language.

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>

> 2. **`tegelema` + `-ga`** is how you say what activity or sport you do — `tegelen jooksmisega`, `tegelen karatega`. The speaking test asks about hobbies and free time every time.

<!-- -->

>

## Chapter 24 — Puhkusele!
*(book pages 190–195 · **Core A2** · [open the chapter →](Nagu-Eesti-24-Puhkusele.md))*

**Teemad / Topics**

| Teema | Sisu |
|-------|------|
| Suvevaheaeg | planning the summer break |
| Reserveerime hotellitoa | booking a hotel room by phone and online |
| Lähen tuppa | checking in, the room, problems with it |
| Reisibüroos | at the travel agency, packages and prices |
| Kindlustusfirmas | travel insurance, claims |

**Keeletark** *(page 194)*

| Grammatika | Näide |
|------------|-------|
| **The four principal parts** | kuula**ma** · kuula**ta** · kuula**b** · kuula**tud** |
| **The impersonal — present** | suvel sõide**takse** rohkem rattaga · õhtuti käi**akse** jõusaalis |
| **negative present** | siin **ei** kontrolli**ta** meie passe |
| **past** | eile kirjuta**ti** mulle Rootsist |
| **negative past** | mu pagasit **ei** kindlusta**tud** |
| **built from the `-da` form** | luge**da**→loe**takse** · teh**a**→teh**akse** · tull**a**→tull**akse** |
| **tundma `keda?` / tunduma `kellele?`** | ma **tunnen** neid inimesi — **mulle tundub**, et … |
| **Two-word verbs** | lahti keerama · lukku panema · põlema panema |

> 🔹 **A2 focus.**
>
> 1. **The impersonal (umbisikuline tegumood) is the last major grammar item in the book.** It is how Estonian says *"one does"* / *"it is done"* — and it is everywhere in signs, rules, news and general statements. You must be able to *recognise* all four forms for the reading and listening papers; simple production is expected too.
> 2. **The four principal parts are what a dictionary actually gives you.** From here on, record every new verb in all four (`-ma`, `-da`, `-b`, `-tud`) and the impersonal, the participles and the past all come for free.
> 3. **`mulle tundub, et …`** is a ready-made opinion opener for the speaking test — softer and more natural than `ma arvan` used repeatedly.

<!-- -->

> ⚠️ **Traps.**
>
> - The impersonal is built from the **`-da` infinitive**, not the `-ma` form: `lugeda → loetakse`, `teha → tehakse`. Starting from the wrong stem gives a nonexistent word.
> - The **negative drops the `-kse`**: `kontrollitakse` → `ei kontrollita`; `lauldakse` → `ei laulda`.
> - The past impersonal `-ti / -di` (`kirjutati`, `lauldi`) looks like nothing else in the language — learn it as its own shape.
> - `tundma` (know/feel, takes the partitive) vs `tunduma` (seem, takes `-le`) differ by one letter: `ma tunnen` vs `mulle tundub`.

**Sõnavara väli:** holidays and booking · hotel and accommodation · travel agency and packages · insurance and claims · the impersonal forms of common verbs.
